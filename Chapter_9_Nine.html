Chapter Five, Part Two. Defeating Death, The Post-2036 Horizon.


Longevity Escape Velocity Futurist Ray KURTS-vile and others propose a concept where life expectancy increases by more than one year per year, effectively allowing people to live indefinitely. Key conditions: Rapid progress: Medical advances coming faster than aging progresses. Cumulative benefit: Each intervention adds more time than it takes to develop A.I. acceleration: Artificial intelligence designing ever-better therapies Whether this occurs depends on the pace of progress in the coming decades. Biological Immortality The furthest reaches of longevity science: Complete aging reversal: Not just slowing but eliminating aging. Digital backup: Uploading minds as biological failsafe. Morpheus Project: Theoretical complete cellular rejuvenation. Ethical frameworks: How society handles indefinite lifespans The Centenarian Society If longevity interventions succeed: Average lifespan of 100 plus becomes normal by mid-century Centenarians as common as octogenarians today Multi-generational families spanning 150 plus years Fundamental rethinking of human life patterns We will not defeat death entirely in the coming decade. Accidents, violence, and eventually the heat death of the universe remain undefeated. But the gradual, inevitable decline we call "natural" aging, the slow accumulation of damage that makes 80 so different from 30-this we can fight. The 2026 to 2036 decade will likely see the first genuine aging interventions approved and available, the first measurable human rejuvenation, and the first serious societal grappling with what longer, healthier lives mean for how we structure our world. We may not live forever. But we may live far longer, far healthier, than any generation before us. The question is not merely whether we can extend life, but whether we can build a world worthy of longer living.
