Chapter 13 Part 2 - The Economic Revolution - The Post-2036 Horizon

Chapter Thirteen, Part Two. The Economic Revolution, The Post-2036 Horizon.


The Post-Work Society If automation progresses sufficiently: Work as choice: Rather than necessity. Purpose beyond employment: Finding meaning in non-economic activity. Leisure civilization: Culture, art, sport, community as focus. Status redefined: Achievement without economic signal. Psychological adaptation: Managing identity without career The Star Trek Economy Science fiction as economic model: Post-scarcity: Replicators and energy abundance eliminating material want. Contribution motive: Work driven by passion and purpose. Resource allocation: Merit and need rather than market exchange. Global governance: Managing shared abundance. The challenge: Transitioning from scarcity to abundance economics Existential Economics The ultimate questions: Meaning without work: What gives life purpose? Status without wealth: How do we signal worth? Identity without career: Who are we if not our jobs? Community without company: Where do we belong? Progress without profit: What drives innovation? The economic transformation of 2026 to 2036 will be driven by technologies that make human labor increasingly optional. This creates both extraordinary opportunity-abundance beyond historical imagination, and profound challenge-distributing that abundance equitably while maintaining social cohesion and individual purpose. The economic system that emerges will likely combine elements of market capitalism, social democracy, and models not yet named. It will require new institutions, new social contracts, and new philosophies of value and purpose. The goal is not merely to produce more wealth but to distribute it wisely, enabling human flourishing in a world where the necessity of labor is no longer universal.
